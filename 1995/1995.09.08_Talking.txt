8/9/95  Talking with men at SÖ -(Many of these men had worked at Gordion 35 yrs. ago).Pre-tractor agriculture and herding - Fifty yrs. ago there was 350-400 HH, about 1,500 people. My father had 500 d. Cereal (kara arpa for animals), now white barley, corn, oats for horses, sugar beet (in 1957), BITTER VETCH*, lentils, chick peas...all  dry agriculture. Water is very little now...Where were the early settlements? near the water? It was very near the water.Fields were ca. 500 m-1 km from the village. Our boundaries are Yassıhöyük, Çekirdeksiz and Müslim, Beycegiz, Avsar...It is a bit rocky and hilly terrain here.. That is only used for grazing. On the flat lands, “YAZI” we do farming.During the growing season, we keep animals on mountains, after harvest they go to fields. On a daily basis, they bring each flock to their owners, they are milked by their owners, and taken back again...the ones who are not milked stay on a 4-5 months basis. Each type is treated differently. Distance is ca. 1-2 km. About 800-1,000 cows; ca. 800 sheep, 400 Angora goats..we have both Karaman and Merinos.*(Bitter vetch -- kara burçak--is not grown any more, since herding is diminishing and it was mainly given as animal feed).What was the proportion of crops in a 500 d. land in the past 50 years?First of all, half of it was left fallow (“herk bırakmak”); rest was planted with cereals; but on the NADAS land we grew lentils, chick peas etc... because it was good nutrition for the soil. It was considered to be still NADAS...Ismail Üstün - is 73 yrs. old. Remembers pre-tractor agriculture and herding.(Worked at YH from 1950-53. ) In 1940s pop. here was   ?  During 1956-60, pop. was ca. 1,800 people; 400 HH. Where did people come from? %5 is from Bolu, Ayas, Avsar, Tatar.. rest is local, Turkmen (Seymen). It is an endogamous village.It is the biggest and oldest village. Earliest settlement was in this center, fields are far. In 1940’s where were the fields? How far? There is a spring which goes to Sakarya. We also did dry farming in mountains, wheat, vetch, barley...Because the springs overflowed, many mosquitoes, floods. So, we farmed in mountains, ca. 2 -3 km from village..it was high, cool, gave plenty of crops.  Two kinds, Kunduru ve gümüs wheat were grown. Now, after 50 years we get similar productivity only if we fertilize and leave NADAS. Because the soil is tired now!...after 50 years...Productivity:  In the past we got 1/10 yarım....1 dekardan 75-100 kg. alırdık.Now a lot of ploughing, cleaning, settling...and we get the same productivity, because the soil is tired. We were 7 people in the family; farmed 60-70 d. Rest of land (40 d.) stayed with NADAS. Technology: We had a pair of oxen, 3 cows, 2 donkeys... According to the number of animals, we planted their feed...of 50 d. of land, 35 d. was wheat, 15 was for barley for animals; rest was planted with chickpea, lentils etc...the early settlers had some irrigated land (20 out of 30)..they used underground wells...but 3/4ths was dry farming, 1/4th was irrigated. Was herding and agriculture practised equally? Agriculture was ahead...If you could feed it by grazing, you would have that many animals.Was herding not an extra capital then? Feed was not available. The most was couple of cows, for milk..How much land is farmed here? 20,000 d. (Only 1,500 d. is irrigated) Grazing: 17-18,000 d. Houses: 1-1,500 d. Now land is divided up, so people leave. This is one factor that productivity falls, as a result of natural movements, death of head of HH...Not due to external factors or war, or conquest.Now there is a move toward animal husbandry. They feed them and resell them; that is profitable...But in general animal husbandry is not what it used to be in the past. Because there were more shepherds to take care of them.We did sell the excess milk, but it was not much...Productivity: A good sheep gives 300-500 grs. of milk/day...One HH needs 10 sheep to take care of its basic subsistence. Above that is sold. Merinos was bred for wool and meat (Merinos is 35 kg; Karaman is 20 kg.). But now wool does not bring much. We have few Karamans...It started with artificial insemination, after 3 generations, it became purebred. The state introduced Merinos males; first we had hybrids (“kırma”).What about angora goat? It has diminished. Goat is not as productive as sheep. So, people stopped it...Tiftik does not yield milk..its only gift is its wool. So, it is not economical to keep it, hire shepherd etc...Let’s say, 40-50 yrs. ago, 3/4 of land was dry farming. Only dependent on water... How did you guarantee the subsistence, in case there was no water.What strategy did you use to protect yourselves? Farmer has no guarantee.I even ate “kenger” ekmegi. It is a kind of thorny plant...we ground the inside, mixed it with flour..when we had drought. We also got aid from the state. Crop rotation was the only strategy for subsistence, plus a few sheep and cows.Plus, working for the 5 aghas who were large landowners.Is there any chance of several close villages to set up cooperatives? to produce and to sell products? Yes, but we  are not able to administer..that’s the problem.  In the history of these villages, there has never been a cooperative.We need a factory which would process our products.. How much land is planted with sugar beets and onion? We used to plant 1,500 d. sugar beets..but that is no longer, because no water. In this village there will be land reform for the first time.Avsar (AR) is ca. 7 km. north of SÖ, up a steep hill which we did on a tractor. It is a mountain village, inhabited by Bosnak immigrants who practice dry farming and animal husbandry.  It is a miracle that they can even farm this rocky, upland terrain. Hakkı Gençdogan and his mother- Village was established over 100 yrs. ago.NOW, there are 9 HH living here. Two HH travel between Polatlı and Avsar during year. His father was born on the way in Amasya, coming from Yugoslavia. (He would have been 100 now)..They came from “Eskipazar” in Yugoslavia.The mother says there were just a few HH when they came.. Biggest time was 25 HH. Prior to that it was a big greek village....She has lived here about 60 years...Crops - wheat, barley, corn, oats, bitter vetch (still grown)...Used oxen..it was dry farming (still dry farming).....Little oats because few herds...Same farming except for tractor; farmed land is limited - cannot expand it; it is 2,000 d. farmed land..One year we plant sunflower, chickpea, lentil, melon on half of it...Next year, we plant wheat and barley on melon fields. We don’t have NADAS. It is all dry farming. How much does it rain? IT IS STILL GOOD RAIN; still we do not have shortages. Used to be more rain and snow; did not use fertilizer. But FERTILIZER is necessary now, to make up for lack of rain and lack of nutrition of the soil.How was it when you first came? Same as now. Grazing land is ca. 2-3,000 d.Did you have more herds before? Yes, much more. Now, only 1 HH hAS FLOCKS. It used to be the “YAYLA” of SÖ village..{Note: Çekirdeksiz was the Yayla of YH, before it was settled}.There are 40-50 cows, ca. 500-600 sheep and goats. Mainly Merinos. What do you do with the milk? We only process it for our own consumption..We can’t market it, because there is not enough of it and we cannot market it.% of butchering , keeping and selling? We sell 70% of males, keep all females.Pre-tractor Days - You farmed the same crops...You wished for rain. How did you guarantee yourself against bad times? Drought? We did not prepare ourselves for bad crops. That is why we farm different crops.  IMPORTANCE OF CROP ALTERNATION against bad years...How much does each HH have? It varies; some 50, some 300 d. I have 150 d. land: 75 d: wheat, barley, rye; 15 d:chickpea; 30 d. sunflower; 15 d. lentil; rest is melon. This way we try to balance...We sell chickpea and lentils and melons..above our needs.This land is very rocky, mountainous...mountains limit our  boundaries. Therefore, we cannot expand. Water is continuous, comes from mountain springs. It is for drinking only, both for men and animals. Herds stay on high land during summer; goats stay all season because we don’t milk them. Milking sheep are brought every evening for milking, then go back to mountain pens.We have Kusburnu, good ahlat..What kind of wheat? Kunduru which is used for good bread flour. Now they use it for macaroni. We don’t plant it anymore, it is not too productive. We plant Rus Bugdayı. Is there any well irrigation here? No, experts say it is 100s of m. below..so we can’t use it. No onion, no sugar beet.This is however, very productive land, even without water. There is coolness here, this soil is very productive. It is even better than that of SÖ.Productivity: 1/10 teneke (1 teneke seed yields 10 teneke wheat) to  1/35, without irrigation.Of course, we work harder to prepare the land...Our tractor parts wear down in a shorter time than that of SÖ.. In one day, they do 50 d., we do 20 d.In the past, we cleared the stones, now we clear them again...Harvesting is also very difficult. Married man live with his parents, in the same courtyard. Of her 8 kids, only 1 son lives here. Do you have ovens? Yes, we had it..because my wife was from Çekirdeksiz, Bulgarian emigre..so she built one in the courtyard...because they make somun, you know. Archaeological implication: So, she introduced that oven to this village.				        PalatinoÄ   '=  '= Ä Ä   '=     E M E Ú˝ YH '95gordionayse  ayse                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  
