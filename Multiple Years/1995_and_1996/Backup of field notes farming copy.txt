The rationale behind its planting so extensively this year is that last year the profit was relatively high..(But then only a few farmers had planted it). Expenses/dönüm:  seed / dek: c. 6-7 milyon TL;  			çapa/ dek: c. 1.5 million TL ; 		plus cost of diesel and electricity and labor for irrigation...In 1995, farmers planted 1,500 dönüm of onion, 1,000 dönüm of sugar beet, on irrigated land. (In Polatlı region, in 1995, 60,000 dek. of land planted with onions.. So there is a big glut. Last year sale price was 10,000; this year, not even demand for 3,000 TL/kg. So, taking last year as a model, many people planted it.! Much product, little demand, therefore, lower price...Why more onion than sugar beet? (1,500 d. onion vs. 1,000 d. sugar beet). Especially, since it requires immediate sale and cannot be stored like sugar beet which gets stored in State silos and investment in onion is higher. But....a) onion yields potentially higher profit; b)onion can be sold privately to middlemen, sugar beet has to go to State; c) for onion they get paid up front, for sugar beet State pays them 6-9 months later. Onion has to be exported abroad, farmers say...ie. FARMERS HAVE TO FIND OUTSIDE MARKETS.  It is even too much for country-wide consumption...Onion is planted in Feb. Soil has to be dry. LAND has to be prepared from Fall. How much water? Does not need much water, but requires constant humidity. Does not need as much water as sugar beet. Onion is very labor-intensive...and very expensive (500 million TL for 50 d.). One person can only do 500-600 sq. m area (1/2 d./day); in sugar beet a laborer can do 1.5 dönüm/day (1,500 sq. m.). Harvest of onion is Sept. 10-15.., then it is let to dry in the fields for a week, then the leaves are cut individually, by hand. Then, store it in sacks.. It is brought back from the fields after drying, laid out in the courtyard, women cut the stalks, separate the small from the bigger ones, place them separately in the 50-kg. sacks and wait for the middleman...Onion is very labor intensive in that it every stage is manual labor....I photographed a courtyard where cutting and bagging were taking place (Orhan Dogan’s house)...Interesting that the dry leaves are used as fuel in oven or tandır for bread....Nothing is wasted...From the point of view of courtyard activities, it differentiates it from the herding courtyard....Sugar beet is less so; the machine cuts the leaves and makes piles, so that it is put on römork and transported to market. Are they harvested at the same time? Yes, if the Sugar Ofis allows you to harvest it early, it happens at the same time...Otherwise, one cannot harvest it when one wants it.. But usually, we wait till November... Then, efficiency goes down by half..in the wet weather, machines work very slowly, labor is inefficient too.				7/7/95  Conversation with Ratıp Esen -Had been Muhtar from 1952-56 and 1968-73. Also worked at Ihtiyar Heyeti...Knows a lot about the history of the village...He was a master carpenter when he came to YH.  He facilitated the building of the new mosque, in 1968. What changes happened since 1930’s in agriculture and herding? I came here in 1936, it was spring, from Kıbrıscık. I came to pay tax/head of sheep which we were sending with a shepherd from K. every year to graze here during winter (Nov.-May). In summer, the flocks came to Köroglu, which is  YAYLA for Kıbrıscık. Then, agriculture was not big here. There were about 15 HH here.In 1940 I came for a wedding of Erdogan Tokat’s uncle.  In 1942 I got engaged to Müyesser. In 1942, State Agricultural Office rented out tractors and other equipment to the farmers at YH. All wealthy resident herders tried out tractors and did wheat..The result in 1942 was so spectacular that Mehmet Ali Aga rented out a whole bar for a day, and paid 4,500 TL. Lots of money back then. (Wheat cost 18-19 kurus/kg.). So, mechanized agriculture started in 1942, there were about 20-25 HH, then.  A little bit of herding, combined with some farming was going on. So, we set up relationships with the residents here. In 1949 tractors were sold to people...One was Fazlı Kayran. In 1950’s, benefiting from Marshall Plan, under Menderes, many people were able to buy tractors, used it on the farm.Farms were not owned in general. Only some fields near Sakarya were owned with deeds, in 1937. Mostly, people farmed wherever they wanted to. Grazing was getting less.. In 1950, due to excavations a lot of people came here as workers, and they settled here. Further, they farmed....The “ Mevki” names were given according to landowners’ names..Land Use: The earliest farmland was near Sakarya.. The related families lived near each other so as to form cooperative labor units. (But now most of them don’t cooperate, with the exception of Köseoglu, Coban and a few others.)Land is farmed, generally, by people who own land and rent it from Hazine. Sharecropping is another strategy used by farmers who do not own tools or machinery. In addition, families who lack labor force will either rent their land to others or engage in sharecropping.Land Acquisition: Prior to 1953 the families farmed as much land as they wanted and wherever they wanted which was mostly near Sakarya. In 1953 the distributed land was random-- people had no choice to select, according to soil quality. They took what they could get. Each HH got 190 dönüm, some fields at one place, others at 2, 3 or more locations. Nobody objected.. It was mostly dry farming. Another way of acquiring land was to farm it for at least 30 years, without a deed, until the State let you have it  (“zilliyet hakkı”). Now, each HH will be eligible to get 40-50 dönüm, with the new distribution. How many sheep did you have? I had 150 sheep, c. 350 dönüm land..Now I sold all the sheep.  Once the population grew, people rented farm from Treasury, farming intensified with the help of technology and fertilizer etc.. How did the people keep up with more and more productivity? They began to find alternative jobs, got education, so did not depend on farming...For ex. We have 13 children, 450 dönüm land..each would get c. 34.5 dönüm..that’s nothing...  Both farmer’s and herder’s lives are very risky:{“Davarın altından yel gecer, bir kısda biter. “}{Bir dolu olur, bütün tarlayı kasıp kavurur}So, choose crops with least risk..e.g. sugar beet cannot be affected by hail.But wheat and barley would be totally destroyed.When there is no hope for expansion, what do you do? You plant the crop that is least risky and most productive..barley is very productive on this soil...That’s what I count on. The best strategy is mixed agriculture. Takviyen olacak, calısacak adamın olacak, kendin de cok calısacaksın. But there is a limit, too.Coban, Karagöz ve Salim Kayran aileleri are very unsociable, they only work and live for money. It is interesting that distribution of families is according to place of origin:from Midas tumulus west toward Ratip Esen’s house, all except 3 HH are from Beypazarı; and people from Kıbrıscık are west of the new mosque.All living in lumps.....Two Kurdish families,  Fikret Beklemez..His father, Ekrem, is Kurdish on father’s side, not Kurdish on mother’s side...Muharrem (Kezban’s husband)is Ekrem’s wife’s  brother..So, that’s two..Kürt Fatma (Caglar).Why are some gardens bigger than others, also, courtyards vary in size?Initially, they    were all 1.5 hectares. In the past we used to send our herds From Beylikköprüto Beypazarı, Muslim, etc. in winter, for 6 months, in summer they came back to our graZing land in village for milking.. In 1944, there was 15 HH in BK.We were all related families there. In 1952, technology changed, with tractor, irrigation etc... with motor..We still made the canals by hand. What was the productivity? With good rain and tractor technology, in 1952, we got 1/25 (for 1 teneke seed, 25 teneke wheat, which means 25x16=400 kg.)The wealthiest people got 200 yarım (3200 kg.), from 40-50 dönüms. With good rain, we got better yield. This is better than today. Last year, though, we had a good produce.. If you irrigate and manure and weed, you can get 1/40 (we irrigated it 3x)....In the past, cereals were dry farmed. Irrigation was only for sugar beet and melons. Without water, one gets less than 1/2...At present, water and labor are the critical factors in mixed farming in the region. As more land goes into cultivation through government grants to individual farmers,  individual field size gets smaller and further away from the major river, Sakarya. Labor shortage is usually  filled by either hired labor from outside or by  members of the  extended family, as  younger people prefer to work in the provincial town of Polatlı or in Ankara. Water is more critical, however, and unless new wells are drilled which is a very costly process, farmers may have to practice dry farming more efficiently, with 3-year crop alternation and good fallow system, as they did in the early to mid 1900’s.  In the past, Crops were local: wheat (Polatlı kösesi, sülhak, topbas--all used for bread-- these were local). “Kunduru” type wheat was used for macaroni--hard wheat.. Present strategy is to diversify crops, legumes (chickpea, lentil, bean, cumin which require a growth period of 5 months, in addition to irrigated cash crops. At present, the latter takes the place of “Nadas”, ie. each year irrigated fields are alternated with cereals and sugar beet or onion. But in the long run even irrigated crops deplete the soil.  What was the tool technology, prior to 1950?	Döven: to separate wheat from stalk	sandıklı: to throw seed in (it is carried by horses)	saban:      to plough	ekin makinesi: to cut; bıcaklı makine, 4 atla cekilirdi;	tırmık: to gather the cut wheat	gözer (elek): to clean the wheat grains from sticks and stones	yaba: a type of fork used to lift the hay	tırpan: to cut grass, crescent blade with wooden handleThen, put grain in sacks..we did not have enough for a carriageful...they were carried on our backs..people did not plant more than 50 d. (max.)....you did  not have the efficiency to do more area in the time we had for preparing and planting.... we had to finish the work before the snows....Did every house have a dibek? No, only a few people had it...This is for making bulgur.. Wheat which has been cleaned is boiled, dried, put in dibek, beaten with “tokmak”, so that its skin comes off. Again, it is cleaned from its impurities...then it is cracked on the hand mill (between two stones).The same wheat is used for bread flour, as well as for making bulgur.The mill set into a wooden structure which Fatma nine’s husband(Veysel) built is used for cracking barley for animal feed.We always crack the barley for feed, otherwise it is not useful for animals.We have a machine for that purpose,  we also lend it to others or rent it to other families.On Dung Piles: in courtyard. A large pile of loose sheep droppings  in front of sheepfold-- this is retrieved from the “outdoor” space where the sheep are resting. It is new and not mixed with urine. That is mixed with ash from our ovens. We use that for fertilizer in the field...The other Kerme is  dung cakes also from sheep droppings, but retrieved from the indoors of the sheepfold, where for a whole year the dung gets harder with urine...that is taken out once a year and made into Kerme...we don’t add straw in it, like Inek Mayısı.Nasuf Köseoglu: 8/8/95 from Beypazarı.He came here in 1932, he was 13 yrs. old. (Fertilizer and seed are put in as mixed). In May, we put “yaprak gübresi”. 15 days later, we put ilac...against weeds.  Harvest is in mid July. We put wheat both in the irrigated and arid lands; if the irrigated fields are watered 3x, then we can get as much as 1:40 (1 teneke of seed will yield 40 teneke of wheat).  Plough the harvested field in Jan-Feb. let it stay for a year...In may, another ploughing (since there is a lot of weeds after rains). In Oct. 1-5, following year, we start seeding......)Barley: is done in the same way, same dates.. Can you rotate wheat with barley? Yes, but the yield is not so good....But each cereal can be rotated with melon fields.In August we harvest lentils, chickpeas, suflowers.....*In Sept. harvest vegetables, melons, apple etc...Onion: It can be put in the cereal field, in Feb. 10-15.  Watering ca. 10x, just like vegetables. Capa 2x, 3x weeding. Harvest is Sept 15. After that, we can put wheat or melons on the onion field. Also, can rotate sugar beet with onion; but you can put onion on the sugar beet field. Sugar beet is seeded in March, put fertilizer in---2x capa; in August harvest--- 8-9 times water,,  3x capa, 2x weed...Bostan: seeded in May 15th; both irrigated and dry. *  Chickpea - Atanur has 50 d.; Fazlı Çoban has 15-20 d. Extending over the ancient settlement and surrounded by burial mounds or "tumuli", the village  includes good arable  land and covers an area of nearly 2,500 hectares (ha.) (village area is about 2.5 ha.). YH is one of The ethnic composition of the original village is Turkoman farmers, a group of seven families who settled in Kısla district in 1890's , three km. north of the present village. The village moved to its present locality in early 1920's, due to the flooding of the river which killed crops and caused malaria. Initially, the optimum grazing land near the village was shared with sheep and goatherders coming from the mountainous Bolu region (about 120 km. to the north). The interaction between the village farmers and sheepherders resulted in an exchange of hired labor for grazing land . The village population gradually grew to include 60 HH, more people arriving from Bolu, Sivrihisar (Eskisehir) and Beypazari (Ankara ), in addition to groups of ethnic Turks from Bulgaria, Yugoslavia, Romania, as part of the settlement policy of the newly forming Turkish State between the 1930 and 1950's. At present, Yassihöyük is the smallest village, with 412 people, among the four villages which are near to it. 	In 1933 fifty hectares of farmed land was owned by 17 HH. Clearing land and canal irrigation continued at a rapid rate until 1953 when 1,000 ha. of land was under cultivation with wheat, barley, lentils, chick peas, rice and flax. With land reforms of 1952 and 1994 an attempt was made to equalize land ownership among the villagers . However, each HH got several small land plots, of a varied size and soil quality, and randomly distributed in different areas. The number of HH continued to increase until it reached 100 HH by mid-1980's, (presently, 80 HH), with the concomitant expansion of the cultivated land which reached 1,600 ha. while  field size/HH shrank. Fifty years ago herding was practised on a much larger scale because it was very arduous to till the soil with the available plough.
